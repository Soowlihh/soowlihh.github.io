About me

It started with fixing the family TV.

Before I wrote a line of code, I was the one who figured out why the computer was slow or why the TV wouldn't connect. Nobody asked me to. I just liked tracking down what was broken until it worked again. Computer science was where that habit led.

I'm drawn to the backend because it's the engineering. The frontend is what people look at. The backend decides who can log in, whose data belongs to whom, and whether the app stays up when it matters. You don't see it, but every app depends on it, and that's the part I want to be great at.

I was born in the Bronx, spent part of my childhood in the U.S., then moved back to Burkina Faso until college. Growing up between two countries taught me to adapt quickly and figure things out on my own.

I'm quiet. I won't be the loudest person in the meeting, but if you hand me something, it gets done, and done better than asked. What I'm proudest of isn't one project. It's everything I've learned by building.

Projects

SpendSmart

2025 – Present

I wanted to learn to build login and data security the right way, so I built an expense tracker around it.

Every query is scoped to the signed-in user. Refresh tokens live in HTTP-only cookies, reset links expire after one use, and each endpoint is rate limited.

What I learnedSecurity only counts if you can prove it. Tests run on every push and check that one user can't touch another's data.

PawPath

An AI dog-walk planner, built by a small team in under 24 hours.

I built the Express data layer for dog profiles and photo uploads on Supabase, and locked down every private endpoint with Supabase Auth and Google OAuth while teammates built the FastAPI and React sides.

What I learnedMoving fast as a team works when everyone owns a clear piece. We won Best in Design out of 20+ teams.

AI Chatbot Agent API

Summer 2025

My first AI agent. I wanted to see how a chatbot uses tools instead of only generating text.

A FastAPI backend serves a LangChain agent on Google Gemini. It looks up locations and weather, and remembers the conversation with LangGraph checkpointing.

What I learnedHow agents call tools and keep memory, and how to set things up so the AI provider can be swapped without touching the code.

House Price Prediction

Oct 2026

My first machine learning model, built to help an investment firm spot underpriced homes.

It predicts sale prices for 1,460 Iowa homes from 79 features. I explored the data with pandas, then trained and compared models on held-out data.

What I learnedThe right model matters. Switching to a random forest cut the average error by 25%, from $31,476 to $23,718.
